Direction A — “Counter”

Airy, one accent colour, near-neutral surfaces, generous whitespace. The interface recedes and the shop’s numbers are the only thing with colour on the screen.

Who it suits

An owner who opens this on a phone between customers and wants one answer fast. Everything is spaced for a thumb and a glance, and nothing competes for attention except the thing that needs doing.

What you give up

Density. Fewer rows fit on a screen, so long lists — the 333-item catalogue, a 40-line purchase order — mean more scrolling than Direction B. It also looks less like a specialist tool.

The tradeoff in one line

Calm and quick to read at the cost of information per screen; it is a break from AIRA’s current ledger identity rather than an evolution of it.
