AIRA · Competitive plan

Beating Shopify and Square where they can’t follow

Conversational analytics stopped being a product this year. What is left is the part neither incumbent can ship: working with the register a shop already owns, and adding up the ones they run at the same time.

Drafted 25 September 2026 Working document — checkboxes save in this browser

01The call

Square AI is free on the $0 tier. Shopify Sidekick is bundled with a $39 plan. Both answer plain-language questions over the shop’s own data and pin charts, and Square added local weather and events data in October. “Ask your sales data a question” is no longer something we can sell.

What we can sell is that neither one exists unless you are on their platform, and neither will ever add a competitor’s revenue to their own. Those are structural, not feature gaps — they cannot be closed by a roadmap on their side.

So: stop leading with the assistant, lead with coverage and consolidation. The assistant is the interface, not the pitch.

03The three wedges

Each of these is something a competitor would have to damage their own business to match.

Coverage

We work when they don’t

Square AI needs Square. Sidekick needs Shopify. Every shop on Clover, Lightspeed, Toast, Heartland, a QuickBooks POS refugee, or a register that exports a spreadsheet gets nothing from either, at any price. That is a large share of established local retail — the shops that bought a system in 2016 and will not re-platform.

Consolidation

One P&L across registers

A shop running Square in-store and Shopify online has two dashboards that will never be added together. load_context already unions capabilities across a tenant’s active integrations, and integrations is unique on (tenant_id, source). The architecture is built; nothing sells it.

Trust

We refuse

CapabilityUnavailable, the per-metric caveats, the digest’s number validator falling back to a plain template. An incumbent has no commercial reason to say “I cannot compute this.” A bookkeeper can trust the month-end packet in a way they cannot trust a chat window.

04Phase 0 — the floor

None of this is differentiation. It is what has to be true before we can put AIRA in front of one paying shop.

  1. Square adapterM

    Best API of the lot, straightforward OAuth, and it is the demo that makes the consolidation story real rather than theoretical. Build it first even though Square sellers already get Square AI free — they are the second half of every multi-register shop.

    New adapter beside api/app/connectors/adapters/registerone.py. Must pass python tasks.py conformance unchanged.

  2. Clover and Lightspeed adaptersL

    This is where the actual wedge is. These shops have no AI analyst available to them at any price, and no path to one that does not involve replacing their register.

    Same contract. Read docs/canonical-model.md first, and declare capabilities honestly rather than optimistically.

  3. Authentication and accountsM

    There is no current-user concept anywhere in the app. Tenant resolution is a slug in the path with nothing checking the caller is entitled to it.

    api/app/http.py — the file already says in a comment that this is where it lands, and every route depending on Shop inherits it.

  4. BillingM

    Per-shop subscription. agent_runs already records tokens and cost per run, so margin per customer is answerable from day one rather than a guess.

    New module. Keep it out of app/analytics — it is our data, not shop data.

  5. Send a real emailS

    NOTIFY_TRANSPORT still defaults to console and the live Resend path has never been exercised. The weekly digest is half the value of the product and it has never left the machine.

    Set the transport to live, real key, real recipient. Dev tenants use .example addresses on purpose — they cannot be delivered to.

05Phase 1 — the differentiation

Three things, in order. Everything else waits.

  1. All your registers, one P&LM

    Make multi-source the headline instead of an implementation detail. One screen showing in-store plus online plus market booth combined, with per-source attribution, and a month-end packet covering all of it. This is the one thing neither competitor can ever ship, and the plumbing is already there.

    Capabilities already union in load_context. Check the sync screen and the month-end packet handle more than one active integration per tenant before building UI on top.

  2. Cost-capture flowM

    Our margin caveats currently tell the owner what is broken. Turn that into the fix: “47 SKUs are missing costs; here are the 12 that account for 80% of your sales.” It converts our most honest weakness into the feature Square charges $49 a month per location for.

    The ranking query belongs in app/analytics; the write path does not. Costs entered here are ours, not the source system’s — standing rule 2 stays intact.

  3. Knoxville local contextM

    Square shipped weather, events and news in October, so this is where the category is going. We already model a con booth as a channel. UT home games, downtown events, conventions, First Friday. “Saturday is down 45%, but Tennessee was away and it rained” is the difference between a useful alert and a noisy one.

    Feeds the anomaly baselines, which already keep a spread per side. Check any threshold change against test_an_ordinary_stretch_is_quiet — it fails if the detector gets noisy.

06Not building

Written down so we stop re-litigating it.

Customer messaging and SMS Already dropped once, for good reasons: prior express consent, a working STOP, A2P 10DLC registration. Notifications are email only. The migration that removed it is reversible if that ever changes, and this is not that day.
A custom report builder That is Shopify’s $399 tier and it is aimed at someone with an analyst on staff. Our buyer wants the answer, not the tooling to derive it.
Anything touching payments or checkout We read customer systems and never write to them. Competing on payments means competing on rates against two companies whose entire business is rates.

07Pricing

Measured cost is about 1.1 cents a question, so a shop asking twenty a day runs roughly $6.50 a month in compute. That leaves plenty of room.

$49–79 a month flat, all locations included. It reads as “the same as Square Plus, but it works with whatever you already have, and a second store does not double your bill.” Per-location pricing is the thing local shops with a booth or a second front complain about, and not charging it is a one-line differentiator.

08Decide tomorrow

Open questions where a wrong assumption costs a week.

Which adapter actually ships first? Square is easiest and demos the consolidation story. Clover and Lightspeed are where the unserved customers are. If there is a specific Knoxville shop we can name as customer zero, their register wins over both arguments.
Do we have customer zero? Everything above is cheaper to decide with one real shop’s data than with two fake ones. Worth an afternoon of asking before it is worth a week of building.
Does the month-end packet survive two integrations? It reconciles to the cent today against one source. Tips already caught us out once. Worth proving on a two-source tenant before the consolidation screen is built on the assumption that it holds.
Self-serve or hand-held onboarding? Phase 11 in the README is “onboarding playbook, not started”. For the first ten local shops hand-held is almost certainly right, and that changes what auth and billing need to do on day one.
